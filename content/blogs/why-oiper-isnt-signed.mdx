title: Apple, let us sign our damn app
description: We want to sign OiPer. Apple's account requirements are blocking us in Bangladesh, and our users get the installation warnings.
date: '2026-09-26'
---

OiPer isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID certificate. We want to fix that. We're blocked before we can even get there.

Apple requires a developer membership to issue that certificate. Getting a membership requires an Apple Account. For us, based in Bangladesh, getting through that account requirement is the problem.

So we have an app to ship and users who want to run it, but we can't complete Apple's prerequisites for signing it.

That's bullshit.

## Our users get the warnings

When macOS blocks an app because it can't verify the developer, the person installing it has to decide whether to continue. That's a terrible first experience.

We end up explaining why OiPer isn't signed and why installing it takes extra steps. An account-access problem becomes a trust problem for our app.

We're frustrated because we actually want to use the signing system. We want users to download OiPer and open it without this mess.

## Apple owns this process

Developer verification has a purpose. If Apple wants developers to verify their identity and submit software for security checks, it needs to make that process accessible.

Being based in Bangladesh should not leave us stuck at the account requirement.

Apple controls access to Developer ID certificates. Apple controls how macOS treats applications without them. It should take responsibility when developers can't get through the process it requires.

We shouldn't have to apologize to our users for being unable to satisfy a prerequisite Apple controls.

Apple, fix the account-access barrier. We want to sign our app.
